New to GIS?

What is GIS, and why does it matter?

GIS, short for Geographic Information Systems, is technology that turns location and map data into real decisions: the software behind planning a hospital, tracking a flood, or routing a delivery in minutes instead of hours. AWiGIS exists to make sure African women are building and leading that work.

Satellite imagery & remote sensing

Pictures of Earth from space, used to track change like deforestation or flooding.

Drones & aerial mapping

Flying cameras that map small areas, like a farm or construction site, in incredible detail.

Digital twins & 3D mapping

Realistic digital copies of real places, used to plan cities before anything gets built.

AI & machine learning

Software that spots patterns in map data faster, and at a bigger scale, than any human could.

Location intelligence

Turning "where" data into business and policy decisions, from store sites to disaster response.

Web & mobile mapping

The interactive maps you already use every day, in ride-hailing and delivery apps.

Who we are

A safe, welcoming space, whatever stage you're at.

We're African women from all over the world who work in, study, or are simply curious about GIS and mapping. No matter your level, from total beginner to 20-year expert, there's a place for you here.

Being part of AWiGIS means making friends who get it, finding mentors, getting honest career advice, working on real mapping projects for your portfolio, hearing about job openings first, and learning about courses worth taking.

Membership itself is just for women, but most of our events are open to everyone, because the more people who understand this field, the better.

The two groups join forces into one continent-wide community, starting with 41 members from Nigeria, 11 from Ghana, and 1 from Burkina Faso.

Quick questions

New to this? Start here.

What even is GIS?

Short for Geographic Information Systems: software that turns maps and locations into tools for real decisions, like where to put a clinic or how to track flooding.

Do I need a GIS job already to join?

Not at all. Students, complete beginners, and "just curious" women are just as welcome as working professionals.

Is it free to join?

Yes, membership is free. You just need to be an African woman, in Africa or the diaspora.

I live outside Africa. Can I still join?

Yes, we welcome African women in the diaspora as well, not just those currently living on the continent.

What do I actually get as a member?

A private WhatsApp community, mentorship opportunities, job and course leads, and invites to events, plus a lot of people cheering you on.
