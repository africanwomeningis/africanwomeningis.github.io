# Sources for the story card facts

One source per country for the fact shown in the hero "story card" on the
AWiGIS site. All 54 facts were independently verified via web search (not
just re-confirmed from the original draft). 6 facts were factually wrong and
have been corrected on the site; 3 more had loosely-worded claims that were
tightened without changing the underlying fact. Everything below reflects
the corrected, currently-published wording.

Where the verifying research turned up an obscure or unstable link (archive
mirrors, random blogs) for an otherwise-solid textbook fact, it's been
swapped here for a more durable, canonical source (usually Wikipedia or an
official body) covering the same claim.

## What was wrong and fixed

- **Algeria**: dropped the false claim of being "the first satellite built by
  an African nation" (South Africa's SUNSAT beat it by 3 years, 1999 vs 2002).
- **Ghana**: dropped "Sub-Saharan Africa's first satellite" (also false for
  the same reason — South Africa 1999, Nigeria 2003 both precede it).
- **Ethiopia**: "ET-SAT" isn't a real satellite name; corrected to the real
  one, ETRSS-1.
- **Mauritius**: "Japanese university partners" wasn't supported; the actual
  partner was the UN/JAXA KiboCUBE programme.
- **Rwanda**: fixed an anachronism — the Rwanda Space Agency didn't exist
  yet when RWASAT-1 launched in 2019.
- **Côte d'Ivoire**: the claimed 2024 satellite launch hadn't actually
  happened; corrected to what's verified (winning a build/launch slot via
  KiboCUBE in 2024).
- **Comoros, São Tomé and Príncipe, Togo**: facts were directionally right
  but used slightly looser wording than the source fully supports; tightened.

## Animal icons

Each country's story-card icon is either its men's national football team's
real/official animal nickname (25 countries), or, where no animal nickname
exists, the single most iconic wild animal associated with that country
(29 countries), each verified with a source. Primary source for nicknames:
[List of CAF national association football teams by nickname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_CAF_national_association_football_teams_by_nickname).

Two picks were overridden from the initial research for better fit: Rwanda
(mountain gorilla, far more globally iconic than the football nickname
"Amavubi"/wasps) and Madagascar (lemur, more globally iconic than the
football nickname "Baréa"/zebu cattle).

Six picks are lower-confidence (reasonable and defensible, but based on
general knowledge rather than an independently verified source): Egypt
(Nile crocodile), Eswatini (rhino), Kenya (lion), Libya (Dorcas gazelle),
Sierra Leone (lion, from the country's name itself deriving from Portuguese
"Serra Leoa" / Lion Mountains), and Tanzania (lion). Worth a second look if
you want zero ambiguity, though none are implausible.

Since these icons are decorative (no animal name is printed on the card),
the accuracy bar is lower than for the text facts above — but the picks
themselves are still real, not invented.

## Population figures

All 54 population figures are exact (not rounded) and come from a single
consistent source: the World Bank's "Population, total" indicator
(SP.POP.TOTL), 2024, pulled directly from the World Bank API
(`api.worldbank.org/v2/country/all/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?date=2024`) so every
country uses the same year and methodology. Each figure can be independently
checked at `https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?locations=<ISO3>`
(e.g. `?locations=NG` for Nigeria). The site shows the exact figure with
"(World Bank, 2024)" next to it.
